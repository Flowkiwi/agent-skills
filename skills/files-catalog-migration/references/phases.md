# Later phases

A later phase applies the client's answers or a new file delivery, in place, to the catalog this migration created: `diff.py` compares the payloads of the regenerated structured files with what the last import wrote (`<dir>/diff.json`), and update workers apply that difference. `$S` is `python3 $SKILL/scripts` and `<dir>` the workspace. Only items recorded `created` in `state.json` are updated, archived or deleted; a reused product or child is never written.

Same hard rules as the first phase: nothing is written before the developer approved the dry-run of the phase, lookup before each write and each retry, state after each step, no bulk operation, at most 6 workers.

## Procedure

Each step is one command or one question; ask one question at a time, always with a default proposal.

1. **Start**: `$S/state.py merge <dir>`, then `$S/state.py phase <dir> --start` (prints the new phase `<n>`, refused while a result file is not merged). From now on, decisions belong to phase `<n>`: the `force_update`, `removed_action`, `confirmed_rekey` and `confirmed_changes` of an earlier phase no longer count.
2. **Client answers**: report each answer of `client-review.md` (`references/review.md` `## Answers`). An answer about a mapping: edit the concerned `mappings/` entries (set `"origin": "client"`, update the `reason`, remove the `question`). An answer about a decision: `$S/state.py decide <dir> --key <key> --value-json '<json>' --origin client`. New files: `$S/intake.py add <dir> <path> --replace` (a new version of a file; without `--replace` for a new file), then `$S/inventory.py <dir>` and summarize the new defects (`references/sources.md`). A changed `key` or `group` re-keys products: step 6.
3. **Regenerate**: `$S/extract.py <dir> all`. New variants without codes: `$S/codes.py fill <dir>/products.json --prefix <codes.prefix> --start <codes.next_start> --sku-pattern "<codes.sku_pattern>"`, the recorded `codes` rules unchanged (`references/products.md` `## Codes`), record the new `next_start`, then `$S/extract.py <dir> prices`. Then `$S/state.py sync <dir>`. Then record every concept file again, with the origin it already had (`client` stays `client`): `$S/state.py decide <dir> --key <concept> --value-file <dir>/concepts/<concept>.json --origin <its recorded origin>`; `$S/dryrun.py <dir>` lists the stale ones with the exact command. The difference reads the decisions: a concept file not recorded again is missed.
4. **Difference**: `$S/diff.py <dir>` (exit 1 when `payload.py` fails for a product: fix the mapping, back to step 3). Show `new`, `changed` (handle and paths), `removed`, `rekeyed`, `moved`, `blocked_moves` and `unmanaged` (reused products and children, never written: say so). A deleted product back in `products.json` (`deleted_present`, e.g. the client reverts a regrouping) is never re-created on its own and blocks the dry-run: ask whether to create it again. Yes: `$S/state.py reset-deleted <dir> --handle <handle>` (only that one: without `--handle` every deleted product becomes `pending` again), `$S/state.py sync <dir>`, then `diff.py` again (it is `new` now; the products that took its SKUs are removed and rekeyed into it, step 6). No: take it out of `products.json` (mapping, back to step 3). A blocked move (a SKU moved to products this migration cannot update: unmanaged, payload error or no written snapshot) stays on its `from` product, never deleted (its SKU would leave Flowkiwi): say so, the developer moves it by hand or fixes the receiver. New products: check their SKUs and barcodes in the tenant first (`references/products.md` `## Tenant collisions`). A hit on a variant recorded in `state.json` (a SKU of `rekeyed` or `moved`, still held by a product of this migration until the deletion pass) is expected: it is not a tenant collision, never adopt or skip for it. Run `diff.py` again after any later regeneration (steps 6 and 7 may revert a mapping): `payload.py --update` reads the changed paths from `diff.json` and the values from the payload now, so `payload.py --update` and `worklists --update` refuse a `diff.json` of another phase or computed from another `products.json` or other decisions (`products.json or decisions changed since diff.py`). `diff.py` clears the conflicts: run step 8 again after it.
5. **Removed products** (`removed`, created handles gone from `products.json`; in a later phase they are not a drift): one question for all of them, "archive (recommended, reversible) or delete?", then `$S/state.py decide <dir> --key removed_action --value-json '{"phase": <n>, "action": "archive"}' --origin developer` (`"delete"`). The `already_archived` handles are written again only with `delete` (a rekeyed one is always deleted, step 6).
6. **Rekeyed** (a grouping or key change, `rekeyed`: an old handle whose SKUs now belong to other products): show each pair (old handle, new or changed handles, SKUs) and explain that a grouping change re-creates those products: the old one is deleted (never archived: its SKUs must be free), then the new ones are created. Confirmed: `$S/state.py decide <dir> --key confirmed_rekey --value-json '{"phase": <n>}' --origin developer`. Refused: revert the mapping and go back to step 3. A blocked rekey (`blocked_rekeys`, into a product this migration cannot update) is archived, never deleted: say so.
7. **Shared elements**: `$S/plan_shared.py <dir> --changes`: show each destructive change with its reason and its products (`destructive`), the plain `updates`, the `unmanaged` keys (reused, never modified) and the `unknown` ones (recorded without spec). Confirm the destructive ones (`$S/state.py decide <dir> --key confirmed_changes --value-json '{"phase": <n>, "keys": ["<key>", ...]}' --origin developer`) or revert the mapping (back to step 3).
8. **Snapshots and conflicts**, before anything is written in this phase: for every handle of `changed` and `removed` (and the `already_archived` ones to delete: rekeyed, `removal` `delete_rekeyed`, or any when `removed_action` is `delete`; `dryrun.py` lists the missing ones), read Flowkiwi with read operations only: `get_product` on its IRI, `call_operation list_product_variants` and `call_operation list_metafields` on `products/<id>`, every page of both lists (`describe_operation` first on both, for their path and pagination arguments). Write `{"product": <get_product response>, "variants": [<every variant>], "metafields": [<every metafield>]}` to `<dir>/tmp/remote/<handle>.json` with a quoted heredoc, copied verbatim, with only the compared fields (product: `title`, `description`, `tags`, `status`, `category`, `attributes`, `collections`; variant: `sku`, `barcode`; metafield: `definition`, `value`). More than 20 handles: one dedicated read-only worker (its prompt: this step, the handles with their IRIs, no write operation, and the block of `references/create.md` `## Worker MCP rules`). The first time on a tenant, compare one response with these keys: a field Flowkiwi returns under another key (attributes, collections) or per locale (metafields: keep the default locale value) is not compared, so tell the developer instead of renaming it. Then `$S/diff.py conflicts <dir>`: show the conflicts (handle, path, written and Flowkiwi values) and ask once whether to force some or all: `$S/state.py decide <dir> --key force_update --value-json '{"phase": <n>, "handles": ["<handle>", ...]}' --origin developer` (`"handles": "all"`). Not forced: those products stay as they are in Flowkiwi (the update run skips them), tell the client. Never run `diff.py conflicts` again once an update of this phase was merged without reading the snapshots again: they predate our writes and every updated field would read as a conflict.
9. **Dry-run**: `$S/dryrun.py <dir>` (`## Dry-run` below), show it with 2-3 samples of `$S/payload.py <dir> <handle> --update` (a changed product with several kinds of changes, a removed one; a sample whose new shared element is not created yet fails on its IRI: show `payload.py <dir> <handle> --dry-run` for it), plus `references/create.md` step 2 for the new products. **Wait for explicit approval.**
10. **Shared elements**, after approval:
    - `updates`: run `describe_operation` on each `operation` before its first call (`update_metaobject_definition`: check whether `fieldDefinitions` must carry the ids of the existing fields, never drop a field by omission), call it on `iri` with `body`, then `$S/state.py shared <dir> --key <key> --iri <iri> --status created --module <module> --spec-json '<entry spec>'`.
    - New elements: the `plan_shared.py` loop of `references/create.md` step 3 (new category paths, options, collections, entries, definitions).
    - A confirmed destructive key that keeps its key (a metafield type change, an attribute type change, a metaobject definition field removed or retyped when `update_metaobject_definition` cannot apply it): the Shopify flow. `$S/state.py undo-plan <dir> --scope shared:<key>` and walk it per `references/undo.md` (show it, confirm, delete, `state.py deleted`), then create it again with the `plan_shared.py` loop, then run `$S/diff.py <dir>` again (the products now point to new IRIs; no product update is merged yet in this phase, so the snapshots of step 8 stay valid: run `diff.py conflicts <dir>` again). A re-created metafield definition and its values are invisible to `diff.py`: write them again with `$S/state.py worklists <dir> --workers 4 --module metafields` (`metaobjects` for a reference metafield) and the module-only run of `references/create.md` `## Concept added after creation`.
    - A confirmed destructive key the products move away from (a removed or renamed category or collection, a removed attribute option, a removed entry): its products are updated first (step 11 moves them to their new element), and `undo-plan --scope shared:<key>` runs after the update passes, per `references/undo.md` (a 409 means still used: report it, do not force).
11. **Products**: first `$S/dryrun.py <dir>` again, which must exit 0 (after step 10 re-ran `diff.py`, it blocks until `diff.py conflicts` ran again); show any change from the approved dry-run and wait for approval again if something changed. Then `$S/state.py worklists <dir> --workers 4 --update`, the update workers (`## Update worker`), `$S/state.py merge <dir>`, repeated until no product is selected (each call prints its `pass` and the `skipped` handles with their reason: show them). Then the undo of the removed shared keys (step 10). Then the normal creation run for the `new` products: `references/create.md` steps 4 and 5 (the pilot is not required when no new kind of product appears: no new option, concept or status).
12. **Final report**: `references/create.md` step 6, plus the updated, archived, deleted, skipped (with their reason) and failed products of the phase (`$S/state.py summary <dir>`: `update_failed` lists the products whose update, archive or deletion failed in this phase with their error; their status stays `created` or `archived`, so they are not in `failed`; retry: `worklists --update --retry-failed`), the products left as modified in Flowkiwi, and the shared changes applied. Then `$S/client_review.py <dir>` (`references/review.md`).

## Dry-run

In a later phase `dryrun.py` adds, after `To create`, a `Changes (phase <n>):` section: `new`, `changed` (up to 10 handles with their paths, then the `moved` SKUs and the `blocked_moves`, kept on their `from` product), `removed` with the action of the phase (`archive`, `delete` or `not decided`), `already archived`, `rekeyed` (`confirmed` or `not confirmed`, the pairs, the blocked rekeys), `modified in Flowkiwi` with the forced count and each handle with its paths, `unmanaged` (with the reused items never written), the handles not compared, and `shared changes` (destructive with the confirmed count, updates, then each one).

It exits 1 on these `Blocking` lines too:

| Line                                                                           | What to do                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `diff.json is missing` or `diff.json is from phase <n>`                        | step 4                                                                           |
| `diff.json was computed from another products.json or other decisions`         | steps 4 and 8 (`diff.py`, then `diff.py conflicts`): something changed since     |
| `payload.py fails for <n> products of diff.json`                               | fix the mapping or a decision, steps 3 and 4                                     |
| `deleted products back in products.json, never re-created: <handles>`          | step 4 (`state.py reset-deleted --handle`, `state.py sync`, `diff.py`)           |
| `snapshots missing: <handles>`                                                 | step 8 (absent, or read before the phase started)                                |
| `conflicts not computed from the current snapshots: <handles>`                 | `diff.py conflicts <dir>` (a snapshot appeared or changed after it ran)          |
| `removed products without decision removed_action`                             | step 5                                                                           |
| `rekeyed products not confirmed: decision confirmed_rekey {"phase": <n>}`      | step 6                                                                           |
| `destructive shared changes not confirmed: <keys>`                             | step 7                                                                           |

## Update worker

**Worklists.** Once the dry-run of the phase is approved: `$S/state.py worklists <dir> --workers 4 --update` (default 4, at most 6). It reads `<dir>/diff.json`, refused when it was computed for another phase (`diff.json is from phase <n>, run diff.py again`), from another `products.json` or other decisions (`products.json or decisions changed since diff.py: run diff.py and diff.py conflicts again`; the phase decisions aside) or while the conflicts step has not run since the last `diff.py` (`diff.json has no conflicts computed since diff.py ran`, `payload.py --update` refuses too; a handle of `missing_snapshots` is skipped), and prints the `pass` it selected and the handles `skipped` with their reason. Repeat worklists, workers (SKILL.md "Running workers") and `$S/state.py merge <dir>` until it selects no product. Each call returns the first pass with work left:

1. `deletions`: the rekeyed products (diff.json `removal` `delete_rekeyed`: always deleted, never archived, and only after decision `confirmed_rekey` of the phase) and the products still holding a SKU that moves to another product (`moved` `from`: their update deletes that variant; a SKU of `blocked_moves` is never deleted, its variant, prices and option values stay). They are merged before any product receiving those SKUs is updated or created: a SKU exists only once in Flowkiwi.
2. `updates`: the other `changed` handles, then the `removed` ones, with decision `removed_action` of the phase (`archive` or `delete`; an `already_archived` handle only for `delete`). A blocked rekey (`removal` `archive_blocked`, listed in `blocked_rekeys`) is always archived, never deleted, whatever `removed_action` says: its SKUs would be lost. A product whose update left `deferred` creations (its new SKU was still held by another product) is selected again once that SKU is free, for a second pass.

Never selected, and listed in `skipped`: the `unmanaged` handles; a changed or removed handle of diff.json `conflicts` (modified in Flowkiwi since the last import) without decision `force_update` of the phase; a deletion of SKUs whose receiver has such a conflict; a product receiving a `moved` SKU this migration never deletes (its `from` is `unmanaged`, or holds it as a reused variant); a failed update (`--retry-failed` to try again); a product whose deferred SKU is still held; a product whose only change is a SKU it keeps (`blocked_moves`). A handle already done in this phase is not selected again, so a stale `diff.json` never repeats an update. Every deletion of a pass goes to the same worker: `state.py deleted` writes `state.json`, one writer at a time.

Then the creations of the new handles: `$S/state.py worklists <dir> --workers 4` (`references/create.md` steps 4 and 5). In a later phase it holds back, in `skipped`, a new product whose SKU is still a created variant of another product: run the update passes first.

**Prompt.** Generate each one with `$S/prompts.py <dir> worker --worker <n> --update` and pass the file it prints verbatim (SKILL.md "Running workers"), never filled by hand. For maintainers: it is the template of `references/create.md` `## Worker prompt template`, with `{{PER_PRODUCT}}` built from three parts, in this order, with `$S`, `$SKILL` and `<dir>` replaced by real paths (workers only see the prompt):

- this note:

  ```
  Update run (later phase): every product of your worklist already exists in Flowkiwi: its IRI is products.<handle>.iri in the state file and its children are its items there (ref names them). It replaces six rules above. The payload command: build every MCP argument from $S/payload.py <dir> <handle> --update (it reads <dir>/diff.json too). The line format: the line format above, with "status": "updated", "archived" or "failed", the product "iri", in "items" only the items created or found in this run, and "deleted": [every IRI deleted or archived in this run]. Exactly one result line per handle: a product deleted by its delete action writes no line when every step succeeded. The state file is read only: the delete action records its deletions with $S/state.py deleted, the only write to it. Never modify or delete: you update, archive or delete the IRIs payload.py --update prints, and nothing else. They are items this migration created. When done, reply with 3 lines only: handles processed, updated/archived/deleted/failed counts, failed handles with a short reason. The MCP rules above still apply.
  ```

- the **Created vs reused** paragraph of `references/create.md` `## Per product`;
- the procedure below, from **Plan** to the end of this section.

**Plan.** For each handle of the worklist, in order: run `$S/payload.py <dir> <handle> --update`. If it fails, fail the product with its message: never guess a value. It prints the `action` and its operations; copy every value verbatim (keep `\uXXXX` escapes as is in the JSON arguments). Only IRIs returned at runtime (new options, option values, variants) are added by you. Run `describe_operation` once on each operation before its first call (path argument names) and adapt the argument names, never a printed value; do not guess. An operation not found under the name used here (`update_variant`, `update_metafield`...): `search_operations` for it, never invent one. After a timeout, an HTTP 5xx or a 429 on a write, read the item again (`get_*` or `list_*`) before each retry (waits and attempts: the MCP rules): an update already applied reads its new value, a deletion already done reads 404. A 404 on a deletion counts as deleted (gone with its parent). A 409, a schema or validation refusal or any other error: fail the product with the error, the items created so far and the IRIs deleted so far.

**`"action": "update"`**, in this order, each group only when printed:

1. `product` (not null): `update_product` on the product IRI with `product.body` as printed. It is a merge-patch: only the printed fields change, `null` clears a field, `attributes` and `collections` are printed whole and replace the existing lists. A `null` for `description` or `tags`: check in `describe_operation` that the field accepts null; if it does not (or the call is rejected for it), send `""` instead.
2. `options.create`: `list_product_options` on the product first. For each missing option, `create_product_option` (`path.productId`, `title`, `position`), then `create_option_value` per missing value (`value`, `position`, `productOption` = the option IRI). Record `product_option` (ref: the title) and `option_value` (ref: `<title>:<value>`) items, module `core`.
3. `options.values_create`: `list_product_options` first; for each value missing under its option, `create_option_value` (`value`, `position`, `productOption` = `option_iri`). Record `option_value` items.
4. `variants.delete`: `delete_variant` on each `iri` (its sale offers go with it). Add the IRI to `deleted`.
5. `variants.update`: `update_variant` on each `iri` with `body`; with `option_values`, also `optionValues` = the IRIs of the named values, the n-th name belonging to the n-th option (from the `option_value` items of the state, or created at steps 2 and 3). When options are added or removed (a renamed option is both), every variant of the product is printed here with its `option_values` and an empty `body`: link it to the values of the new options, before step 7 deletes the old ones.
6. `variants.create`: for each, `list_variants` with `query.sku = <sku>` (partial match: only a returned `sku` exactly equal counts). On this product: record it per the created/reused rule. On another product: fail the product with a clear error naming that product (`payload.py --update` already left out, in `deferred`, every SKU waiting for a deletion of this migration: a printed creation never waits). Not found: `create_variant` (`path.productId`, `sku`, `barcode`, `optionValues` as in step 5). Record `variant` (ref: the SKU), module `core`.
7. `options.values_delete`, then `options.delete`: `delete_option_value` on each `iri`, then `delete_product_option` on each `iri`. Add the IRIs to `deleted`.
8. `metafields.delete`: `delete_metafield` on each `iri`. `metafields.update`: `update_metafield` on each `iri` with `body`. `metafields.create`: `call_operation list_metafields` on `products/<id>`: a metafield with this definition exists -> record it per the created/reused rule; else `call_write_operation create_metafield` `{"path": {"ownerResource": "products", "ownerId": <id>}, "body": <entry body>}`. Record `metafield` with module `metafields` (`metaobjects` for a reference metafield), `"ref"` = the entry `key`, `"shared"` = the entry `shared`.
9. `prices.delete`: a sale offer has no delete operation: `update_sale_offer` on `iri` with `{"status": "/api/statuses/ARCHIVED"}`, except an offer of a variant deleted at step 4: it is already gone, do not call (a 404 there counts as deleted too). Add the IRIs to `deleted`. `prices.update`: `update_sale_offer` on each `iri` with `body`. `prices.create`: replace `<variant IRI of <sku>>` in the body with the IRI of that variant (state item or step 6), `call_operation list_sale_offers` with `query.variant` = that IRI and `query.country` = the body `country`: an offer exists -> record it per the created/reused rule; else `call_write_operation create_sale_offer` `{"body": <entry body>}`. Record `sale_offer` with module `prices` and `"ref"` = `<sku>:<country code>` of the entry.
10. `deferred`: nothing to call. Each SKU is still a variant of `held_by`; a later pass creates it, with its prices, once that deletion is merged.

Line: `"status": "updated"`, even when only `deferred` was printed.

**`"action": "archive"`**: `update_product` on the product IRI with `product.body` (status ARCHIVED). Line: `"status": "archived"`, `"items": []`, `"deleted": []`.

**`"action": "delete"`**: walk `steps` in order (children first), each with the operation of its `kind`: `sale_offer` `update_sale_offer` with `{"status": "/api/statuses/ARCHIVED"}` (skip it when its variant is deleted in this run: it goes with it), `metafield` `delete_metafield`, `variant` `delete_variant`, `option_value` `delete_option_value`, `product_option` `delete_product_option`, `taxonomy_link` and `collection_link` nothing (they go with the product), `product` `delete_product` (refused with 409 while a variant remains). A 404 counts as deleted. Once the product is deleted, every step IRI is deleted (its children went with it): write them as a JSON array to `<work dir>/deleted-<handle>.json` with a quoted heredoc, then `$S/state.py deleted <dir> --iris-file <work dir>/deleted-<handle>.json`, and write no result line. A step refused (409 or another error): stop, record the IRIs deleted so far the same way, then a `"status": "failed"` line with the product `iri`, the error and `"items": []`.
